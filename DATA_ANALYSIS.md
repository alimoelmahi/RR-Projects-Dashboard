## Metric Rules

1. **Numeric extraction:** only whole numeric cells/strings are used; narrative quantities such as `1000 (635 F)` or arithmetic strings such as `2267+100` are not silently interpreted. Missing is null, not zero.
2. **Activity actual priority:** CERF uses Actual_Jan then Actual; EAA uses Q4_Cum then IP_Cum_Actuals_Q3; Italy uses numeric Achievement; JSB uses numeric IP Actual_04.05.26 then IP Actual_Nov; Kuwait uses the observed latest numeric activity-actual column then Actual (10.02); NKA uses Actual_Total; SDC livelihoods uses the rightmost cumulative Total then preceding cumulative totals. These are source-column priorities, not a synchronized reporting date. The chosen column label and row remain traceable in each activity record. Newer narrative data is retained in the source audit, not converted to a numeric completion value.
3. **Activity index:** unweighted mean of available numeric actual/target ratios, capped between 0 and 100%. Unknown and nonnumeric rows are excluded from that mean; measured/total counts remain visible. Reaching 100% on measured rows does not certify award completion.
4. **Derived status:** complete requires all extracted activity statuses to be complete. Other labels use source activity evidence and award dates. Planning workbooks are distinguished from implementation evidence. Unknown does not mean planned or completed.
5. **RAG:** Red means award end passed without complete extracted activity evidence. Amber means missing/ambiguous reporting date, factsheet update older than 90 days, or unknown status. Green is reserved for the remaining cases. These are management screening rules, not formal assessed risk ratings.
6. **Milestones:** source handover dates are observed milestones. Award end dates are closure follow-up deadlines, not invented activity deadlines. No reliable project-wide milestone baseline or critical path is supplied.
7. **Geographic filters:** identify associated award records and filter mapped observations. Award allocation and activity indices remain award-wide. There is no defensible regional budget allocation or locality-specific activity percentage for every award.
8. **Period filter:** selects award-date overlap with a quarter. Time series shows scheduled concurrent awards, not a fabricated history of performance, spending or beneficiaries.
9. **Beneficiaries:** site estimates are separated into people and households. No household-to-person multiplier is applied. Demographic and indirect categories can overlap and are not summed into unique reach. Target coverage percentages cannot be verified consistently.
10. **Finance:** currencies remain separate. Planned workplan costs are not award totals. Reported financial actuals remain labelled with their source basis; no pillar burn rate or utilization ratio is asserted.

## Geographic Processing

Coordinate pairs use WGS84 `[longitude, latitude]`. Unambiguous decimal prefix/suffix cardinal and degree/minute/second strings are parsed and validated against a Sudan bounding region. Reversed axes, missing cardinal components, ambiguous numeric encodings and missing coordinates remain flagged, not silently corrected.

Source boundary rings use the opposite winding to D3's spherical geometry convention. Rings are reversed in the generated working copy only; the original layer is unchanged. A point-in-polygon test verifies the known Madani site falls in Al Jazirah. State aliases reconcile Aj Jazirah/Al Jazeera/Gezira, Gadaref/Gedaref and other observed variants.

The earlier water CSV links by explicit donor label; NKUWAIT links to NKA rather than the earlier Kuwait award. Its within-award observations within approximately 64 metres may be merged as potential duplicates. The added 252-row register is decoded as Windows-1252, linked to existing awards by donor, and retained row-by-row without deduplication, including five repeated coordinate pairs. It reports 231 valid coordinate pairs, 220 rows marked coordinates provided, and 196 BOQs received; 21 rows have no valid coordinates. Eleven rows contain coordinates while marking them as not provided; sixteen spatial/metadata flags from this source are retained for review. Its status values describe both delivery and documentation readiness: only `Delivered` and `In Progress` map to implementation statuses; map preparation, draft map, revisions and incomplete SOW labels remain source-register statuses, not project progress. Different-award co-located sites remain separate, so beneficiary estimates can still overlap.

The two source-versus-boundary state flags are **Galgani Area** (source Sennar, boundary Blue Nile) and **Arkawit Area** (source Al Jazirah, boundary Sennar). Confirm GPS values, boundary vintage and administrative assignment before changing either source.

Map modes distinguish project points, dynamic screen-grid clusters, estimated service instances per 1,000 source population, and contextual needs severity. A cluster is concentration, not a statistically significant hotspot. A locality with no selected project observations is not necessarily unserved. Density denominators mix UNFPA 2024 overrides and boundary populations of unspecified reference year; the tooltip and source notes identify this limitation.

Health facilities, local schools, HOTOSM water/education, education facilities and POI layers are contextual references. Non-point reference geometries are displayed as D3 centroids, with polygon winding normalized in a working copy; these centres are not project locations. The 2,437-row education-authority register contains locality codes and reported school attributes but no point coordinates; its locality aggregates and raw reference are exposed without invented school points. The national school workbook is fully audited separately. No shapefile was present in the discovered project files.

Humanitarian context retains its local source attribution: IOM DTM 30 July 2026 displacement snapshot and OCHA JIAF 2026 needs/severity. These are supplied snapshots, not independently refreshed or verified live services.

## Analysis & Vulnerability

The GIS workspace has a dedicated **Analysis & Vulnerability Assessment** tab. The supplied humanitarian indicators support locality IDP/returnee totals, JIAF severity and sector PiN; project-site observations support presence/coverage gaps. These are linked by the supplied ADM2 locality codes and validated project coordinates. The displacement inputs are a single IOM DTM snapshot (30 July 2026), not a movement-flow or historical trend series. State summaries aggregate locality snapshots; IDP/returnee categories may overlap and should not be interpreted as unique combined people.

No ACLED event dataset was present in the workspace. The app accepts a local, authorized ACLED CSV export using standard columns such as `event_date`, `event_type`, `fatalities`, `latitude`, `longitude`, `admin1` and `admin2`. It parses and spatially matches the file in browser memory only; events are not transmitted or persisted, and a browser refresh clears them. Rows with valid dates/types but no locality match remain in event totals/trends but do not appear as mapped locality exposure. A 50 MB import limit applies. Check ACLED terms and source-period completeness before using an export.

The current integrated vulnerability score is explicitly **provisional**, not a validated JIAF severity classification. It combines capped conflict-event rate (30%), displaced population per 1,000 population (20%), JIAF severity normalized to five (20%), maximum sector PiN share (15%), and an inverse mapped-site count (15%) when locality-matched ACLED is loaded. Without it, weights are renormalized across displacement (30%), severity (30%), PiN (25%) and site gap (15%). Missing dimensions are omitted and remaining weights renormalized. Scores map to Low (<0.30), Medium (0.30–<0.55), High (0.55–<0.75) and Very High (>=0.75). Accessibility constraints are not scored because no accessibility layer is supplied. The inverse site count is a rough coverage proxy, not intervention quality, capacity, beneficiary reach or unique facility presence.

The ACLED monthly chart, event-type bars, fatality estimates, conflict choropleth and recent-risk screen are blank/unavailable until a dataset is imported. The increasing-risk screen compares the latest 90 days with the preceding 90 days, requiring at least three recent mapped events and a greater-than-20% rise; it is a triage flag, not a forecast. Date filters apply to ACLED events only, not to the one-time displacement snapshot. The assessment includes source caveats in the interface and highlights localities with high provisional scores and no mapped intervention site for verification, not an automatic resource-allocation instruction.

## Management Recommendations

1. Request signed completion/acceptance documents and updated closure evidence for CERF, EAA, JSB and Kuwait before treating passed award dates as successful closure or confirmed delay.
2. Reconcile award budgets, currency denomination, commitments and expenditure against finance. Add ledger reporting dates and accounting definitions before publishing utilization KPIs.
3. Resolve the 120 unmapped observations and 27 spatial validation flags. Reconcile coordinate metadata in the added register; prioritize intervention points over reference-facility geocoding and preserve original coordinate text with a correction audit trail.
4. Request dated trackers for missing/ambiguous updates and refresh those older than 90 days. Separate current actuals, cumulative actuals, period targets and forecast completion in the next reporting template.
5. Adopt explicit indicator IDs, units, reporting dates, beneficiary deduplication rules, disaggregation definitions, milestone deadlines and verified project closure states across awards.
6. Validate high-severity/low-observation localities with field teams and partner coverage information before calling them underserved or reallocating funding.
7. Operationalize REVIVE's source risks around conflict/access, GPS-tagging restrictions, social tensions in beneficiary selection and inflation. Assign formal risk owners, mitigation actions, probability/impact ratings and review dates; monitoring responsibilities alone are not that register.

## Delivery and Assurance

The application provides linked D3 visualizations, mapping, source drill-downs, rule-based recommendations, data-quality exceptions and CSV exports. It is a local, read-only analytics platform. Authentication, access control, hosted persistence, collaborative editing and scheduled data refresh require a separate deployment implementation.

Automated checks cover normalization, currency separation, source coverage, D3 polygon winding, entity relationships and source-risk separation. Desktop/mobile browser tests cover all workspaces, filters, charts, map controls, reference layers, details, exports, viewport containment and nonblank map pixels. These checks validate software behavior, not the underlying truth of workbook statements.
